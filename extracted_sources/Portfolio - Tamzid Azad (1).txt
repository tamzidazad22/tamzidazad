Background:
I had spent more than 2 years designing, deploying and driving growth of OTT & IoT products while working as Product Manager at two 
of the largest digital services provider of Bangladesh. A turning point came in 2017, when I saw a poster of ‘Ignite’ (global incubator 
hosted by Telenor group among the employees of it’s offices in 13 countries) in our office. I was quickly motivated to participate. I 
needed two other person to form a team. With some effort, I managed to persuade two of my colleagues to team together with me. In 
January 2017, after 3 stages of initial screening, we made it to the top 10 out of 180 global teams. 
3 days Design Sprint workshop conducted by Fresh Tilled Soil. 
During the program we were sent to Norway, Singapore and Myanmar to attend trainings on how to build a product from scratch. We 
eventually ended the program as Global Champions. After the incubator ended, our mission to launch this as a commercial product started.

## Page 4
The problem: In many places of the world, especially in Southeast Asia, fuel theft committed by chauffeurs and fuel pump workers is a 
chronic problem. In Bangladesh only the Dhaka North City Corporation lost $20M in 2015 alone from fuel theft and receipt forgery. The
problem was this acute because the existing methods of tracking fuel costs were manual, done with pen and paper and based on trust.
The solution: Our idea was to tackle fuel theft from vehicles and pumps using IoT. The objective was to enable fleet managers with real 
time data(collected through a sensor placed in vehicle) on amount of fuel present in his/her vehicles.
The journey of turning this idea into a product can be divided into 3 parts-
1. Knowing the users
2. Getting validation through Low fidelity prototypes
3. Development and Deployment
Live incidents of fuel theft in Dhaka , A chauffeur, Abdus Salam(31) confessing he has done it multiple times himself, Interview with a fleet manager to know more about the pain points


## Page 5
1. Knowing the users: 
Before building anything we wanted to cover perspectives of all 
stakeholders involved. We started our exploratory research to build 
user personas. After a month we could come up with three key 
personas. We built empathy maps and user journeys for all the 
personas to grasp a better understanding on their pain points. It was 
also clear from the research that fleet managers will be our primary 
users as drivers accountability is based on the real time data.
User personas 
2. Getting validation through Low fidelity prototypes:
Fleet managers generally have average to below average 
tech savviness. A key insight was that they need fuel data 
mostly to make reports that they use to pay bills to fuel 
pumps. So in our first prototype, we made the simplest 
webpage where they could download reports from. 
We didn’t have real time data or any backend then. So we 
had to manually collect data and put in the webpage 
everyday for the fleet managers to download. This gave 
them an almost real time feel. And this resulted into an 
overwhelming validation. Fleet managers testified that the 
major pain of not being able to answer to management and 
prevent theft/forgery of fuel receipt could be tackled if they 
had this data.


## Page 6
2. Development and Deployment
With the validation from fleet managers, we embarked into development. We hired a developer, took a device partner onboard and made 
our squad for development. We followed the SCRUM methodology and agile principles. 
We hit a major speed bump while designing technology architecture. In our research, fleet managers said they were sensitive about putting 
an external hardware inside the vehicle fuel tank due to security concerns. So we had to pivot and spend another month of RnD and testing 
to come up with a new technology that could tell the amount of fuel present in a vehicle tank without putting any sensor inside the fuel 
tank. We developed the front in 5 iterations keeping it aligned with continuous feedback from fleet managers. With the backend already set 
up, the solution was ready for deployment

Results/Impact:
We released the beta version of the product in July 2018. And to-date it has saved 32% cost 
by preventing/tracking fuel theft in 4 fleets where we deployed the solution. 
Taking an idea and turning it into a product and in effect providing a solution for a big social 
problem has been the most fulfilling experience of my career so far. It was a cherry on top 
to watch it being recognized when Eagle Eye won 2nd Prize(Best Technology Innovation) in 
Bangladesh Innovation Awards 2018.
